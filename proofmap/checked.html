The proofs, the Lean and every program were written by an AI system working with Justin Roughley, and the paper has not been peer reviewed. This page says what has been checked, by what, and what has not.

By Lean

The combinatorial heart of the proof is formalised in Lean 4, and Lean’s kernel checks every step. In a combinatorial form, in which a patch of tiles is described by which edges are glued, the Lean proves that every supertile has the signature (); that in the union of any chain of supertiles in which every edge is eventually glued, every strand is two-way infinite (), and that the nest is such a chain; that the nest tiling, grown outwards from a single tile, each block sitting in the same place (child number 1) inside the next, is one two-way infinite strand (), with the bounds of , which are exact; and that every vertex of a tile of a block of the nest is surrounded by the block two levels up (, at the vertices of tiles). Its kernel also re-runs the finite computations these use.

The build fails unless the main theorems rest only on Lean’s three standard axioms, each proves exactly its claim in the statement file, that file and the files it imports are unchanged since their SHA-256 was recorded, and leanchecker, replaying every declaration through the kernel, finds no problem. The public repository’s checks also run comparator, the Lean developers’ tool for judging whether proofs prove exactly the statements of a trusted file: what is proved.

The Lean does not cover the link to actual tilings of the plane: that the supertiles of a real spectre tiling are glued as the tables say, that a tiling whose supertiles fill the plane is the union of such a chain, and that the nest tiling fills the plane. The paper argues these in words; together they are the assumption (M) written at the top of the statement file: what the Lean checks, and what it does not.

Whether the right thing has been proved comes down to reading the statement file: Statement.lean, line by line. Every Lean file in full: the Lean.

By computer

Every computation the proof relies on is run by paper/code/run_all.sh, and has been re-run in full, with its logs kept: every step of the run, with its log. Each result marked computed names the program that checks it: which script checks which statement.

The geometric computations were done twice, by two separate programs, one in floating point and one in exact arithmetic; like every program here, both were written by the AI. A further check runs Kaplan’s implementation of the spectre’s substitution, the program of the spectre authors’ interactive application, and finds the paper’s Table 1 and, tile for tile, the same supertiles up to level 4. Beyond what the proof needs, every supertile of every kind was assembled by computer up to level 9, about 133 million tiles, and no strand closes.

Every tool used, with its version, is listed in the paper’s Tool and computational resource disclosure, in Appendix B.

By the AI

The AI reviewed the written argument several times, and the errors it found were fixed.

By a human

Justin Roughley has read parts of the paper and spot-checked pictures and counts.

What has not been checked

The paper has not been peer reviewed. Most of the proof has not been reviewed by a human, and no professional mathematician has reviewed it. No human has checked that the Lean’s statements say what the theorems say.
